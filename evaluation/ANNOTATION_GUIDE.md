## Goal

Label 200 Uber customer support tweets with two ground-truth fields:
1. **Intent** — what the customer is trying to accomplish.
2. **Escalation** — whether a human agent must handle this.

Your labels become the evaluation standard. Be consistent, deliberate, and honest about uncertainty.

## How This Sample Was Built

### Source

All 200 examples come from the **held-out pool** of 200 Uber_Support conversations that were excluded from training, dev, and retrieval indexing via temporal splitting.

### Sampling Method

1. **Original pseudo-labels** were assigned by keyword matching (the `auto_intent` column).
2. **Reclassification** was applied: messages with strong customer-initiated cancellation signals were relabelled as `cancellation`; messages with explicit refund requests were relabelled as `refund_request`. This was done because the original keyword matcher assigned 0 examples to `cancellation` and only 2 to `refund_request`.
3. **Stratified sampling** was performed with target counts per intent, then remaining slots were filled from the largest available groups.
4. **Randomized ordering** — examples are shuffled, not sorted by `auto_intent`.

**Random seed:** 42 (reproducible via `evaluation/rebuild_golden_set.py`).

### auto_intent Is Not Ground Truth

The `auto_intent` column is a **sampling aid only**. It was generated by imperfect keyword matching. Your human labels (`human_intent`) are the ground truth. Disagree with `auto_intent` whenever your judgement differs.

## Intent Taxonomy

### 1. `ride_status`

**Definition:** Questions or complaints about the ride itself — driver location, wait times, pickup/dropoff, ride completion, driver cancellations, wrong routes, lost items in vehicles.

**Belongs here:**
- "Where is my driver?"
- "My driver cancelled on me again."
- "I left my phone in the car."
- "The ride ended early and I'm still on the highway."

**Does NOT belong:**
- Fares or charges for the ride → `trip_fare_dispute`
- App crashes preventing ride tracking → `app_functionality`
- Driver was rude (no ride-status issue) → `complaint`

**Distinguishing from `app_functionality`:** If the app is broken but the complaint is about the ride experience (e.g., "driver went the wrong way"), label `ride_status`. If the app itself is the problem (e.g., "GPS shows wrong location"), label `app_functionality`.

**Distinguishing from `trip_fare_dispute`:** If both ride and fare are mentioned, pick the PRIMARY issue. "My driver took a wrong route AND overcharged me" — if the charge is the main point, `trip_fare_dispute`. If the route is the main point, `ride_status`.

---

### 2. `account_access`

**Definition:** Cannot access account — login failures, banned/deactivated accounts, OTP/verification issues, password resets, phone/email changes.

**Belongs here:**
- "Can't log into my account, not getting the verification code."
- "Why is my account banned?"
- "I need to change my phone number on my account."
- "App says my password is wrong but I just reset it."

**Does NOT belong:**
- App crashes during login → `app_functionality`
- Wanting to delete the account → `cancellation`
- Ride issues that mention account → `ride_status`

**Distinguishing from `app_functionality`:** If the app won't let them log in (wrong password error, verification loop), that's `account_access`. If the app crashes or is buggy in general, that's `app_functionality`. Ask: "Is the problem with their account, or with the software?"

**Distinguishing from `cancellation`:** "I want to cancel my account" = `cancellation`. "I can't access my account" = `account_access`.

---

### 3. `app_functionality`

**Definition:** The app or website is broken — crashes, bugs, GPS errors, map issues, features not working, update problems.

**Belongs here:**
- "App crashes every time I try to open it."
- "GPS shows me in the wrong location."
- "The map won't load."
- "Promo code field is greyed out."

**Does NOT belong:**
- Account login issues → `account_access`
- Ride problems caused by app → `ride_status` (if the ride is the primary complaint)
- Fare display bugs → `trip_fare_dispute`

**Distinguishing from `account_access`:** See `account_access` above.

**Distinguishing from `ride_status`:** "App crashes while tracking my ride" — if the tracking/ride is the concern, `ride_status`. If the crash itself is the concern, `app_functionality`.

---

### 4. `trip_fare_dispute`

**Definition:** Disputing a charge — too much surge, double charged, fare doesn't match estimate, incorrect receipt amount. The core issue is money.

**Belongs here:**
- "I was charged $36 for a trip that normally costs $11."
- "Charged twice for the same ride."
- "The surge was 3x but the app showed 1.5x."
- "Why was I charged $140 for a 3.91 km trip?"

**Does NOT belong:**
- Explicitly asking for money back → `refund_request`
- Ride issues without a fare complaint → `ride_status`
- General payment method issues (card declined) without ride context → `other` or `account_access`

**Distinguishing from `refund_request`:** "I was overcharged" = `trip_fare_dispute`. "I want my money back" = `refund_request`. If the message does both, ask: "Is the PRIMARY intent to dispute the charge, or to get a refund?" Dispute = `trip_fare_dispute`. Refund = `refund_request`.

**Distinguishing from `ride_status`:** See `ride_status` above.

---

### 5. `complaint`

**Definition:** Expressing dissatisfaction — rude driver, bad experience, frustration — without a specific actionable request. This is a vent, not a task.

**Belongs here:**
- "Worst experience ever. Driver was rude and the car was filthy."
- "I'm done with Uber. Never using this again."
- "Terrible service, food was cold."
- "Your driver made inappropriate comments."

**Does NOT belong:**
- Complaint ABOUT a charge → `trip_fare_dispute`
- Explicitly asking for money back → `refund_request`
- Ride status issue (e.g., driver took wrong route) → `ride_status`
- Safety emergency → escalate (but still label the intent)

**Distinguishing from `other`:** `complaint` = negative sentiment with a clear complaint. `other` = neutral/general message. "Your app is slow" could be either — if frustrated, `complaint`. If matter-of-fact, `other`.

**Distinguishing from `refund_request`:** "This is terrible, I want a refund" — the refund is the actionable request, so `refund_request`. "This is terrible" (no request) = `complaint`.

---

### 6. `refund_request`

**Definition:** Explicitly asking for money back — refund, return my money, give me credit, reverse the charge. The primary intent is getting money returned.

**Belongs here:**
- "I want a refund for this ride."
- "Give me my money back."
- "Can I get credit for the cancelled order?"
- "Please refund the extra charge."

**Does NOT belong:**
- Questioning a charge without asking for refund → `trip_fare_dispute`
- Venting without asking for money → `complaint`
- Canceling an account → `cancellation`

**Distinguishing from `trip_fare_dispute`:** See `trip_fare_dispute` above. The key word is "explicitly asking." If they say "refund," "money back," or "return my money," it's `refund_request`.

---

### 7. `cancellation`

**Definition:** Customer wants to cancel something — a ride, their account, a subscription, an Uber EATS order. Also includes complaints about cancellation fees.

**Belongs here:**
- "I want to cancel my ride."
- "How do I delete my Uber account?"
- "I need to cancel my EATS order."
- "Why was I charged a cancellation fee?"

**Does NOT belong:**
- Driver cancelled on the customer → `ride_status`
- Can't access account (but not trying to delete) → `account_access`
- Asking for refund after cancellation → `refund_request`

**Distinguishing from `ride_status`:** If the DRIVER cancelled, that's `ride_status`. If the CUSTOMER wants to cancel, that's `cancellation`.

**Distinguishing from `refund_request`:** "Cancel my ride and refund me" — if refund is the primary ask, `refund_request`. If cancellation is the primary ask, `cancellation`.

---

### 8. `other`

**Definition:** Does not fit any other intent. General questions, positive feedback, spam, off-topic, or truly ambiguous messages.

**Belongs here:**
- "Thanks for the quick response!"
- "Do you operate in my city?"
- "How does Uber POOL work?"
- "Great service today!"

**Does NOT belong:**
- Any message that fits one of the 7 specific intents above

**When to use:** Only when no other intent fits. Do NOT use `other` as a fallback for difficult decisions. Commit to the best-supported intent.

## Escalation Criteria

**Question to ask:** Would a human agent meaningfully add value beyond what an automated template response could do?

### Escalate (`True`)

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| Safety/security concerns | "Driver was following a dark alley, I felt unsafe" |
| Potential fraud/unauthorized activity | "Someone used my account to book a ride I didn't take" |
| Account-level investigation needed | "My account was wrongly banned" |
| Legal/regulatory issues | "I'll be contacting my lawyer" |
| Complex situations requiring human judgment | "I was in an accident during the ride" |
| Lost items in vehicles | "I left my wallet in the car" |
| High-value disputes (>$50) | "Charged $200 for a $20 ride" |
| Information insufficient to resolve safely | Details too vague for any template to address |

### Do NOT Escalate (`False`)

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| Standard status checks | "Where is my driver?" |
| Simple fare questions | "Why was I charged $5 extra?" |
| Routine account updates | "I need to update my email" |
| App bug reports (non-critical) | "Map is a bit slow to load" |
| Positive feedback | "Great ride, thanks!" |
| Simple refund requests | "Can I get a refund for this ride?" |
| General inquiries | "Do you accept cash?" |
| Negative sentiment alone | "Terrible experience" (vent only, no safety/fraud) |

**Important:** Negative sentiment alone does NOT warrant escalation. A customer venting about a bad ride is `complaint` + `False`. A customer reporting unsafe driver behavior is `ride_status` + `True`.

---

## Ambiguity Policy

1. **Choose the best-supported intent.** If a message touches multiple intents, pick the one with the strongest evidence in the text.
2. **Never invent new intents.** Use only the 8 defined intents.
3. **Use `annotator_notes` when genuinely ambiguous.** Explain your reasoning.
4. **Do not use `other` for short messages.** "Charged twice" is short but clearly `trip_fare_dispute`. Short ≠ ambiguous.
5. **If truly stuck, use `other` and explain.** Write why in `annotator_notes`.

---

## Practical Tips

- **Batch by intent.** The CSV is grouped by `auto_intent`. Work through one group at a time for consistency.
- **Go with your first instinct** for clear cases. Save deliberation for genuinely hard ones.
- **Check your escalation rate.** 5–15% is typical. Over 20% likely means over-escalating.
- **Reference `auto_intent`** as a starting point, but override when you disagree.
- **Take breaks.** Work in chunks of 50 to maintain quality.
